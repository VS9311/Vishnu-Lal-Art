# Vishnu Lal — Visual Identity & Art Direction System V1.0

## Visual Thesis
**The Archive should feel discovered, not decorated.**

Draw from paper, charcoal, photographic documentation, archival notation, measured typography, negative space, scale and silence. Do not draw from generic luxury aesthetics.

## Material World
Paper: warm, imperfect, tactile.
Charcoal: black, particulate, irregular, physical.
Graphite/ink-like notation: secondary documentary language.
Screen: a window through which the physical work is encountered.

Suggest materiality without fake paper textures or decorative effects.

## Colour
- Archive Black — deep near-black
- Paper — warm off-white
- Charcoal — softened black/grey
- Dust — restrained grey

No gold, metallic gradients, jewel tones or ornamental accent colours in the core identity.

## Typography
Display/editorial: high-quality restrained contemporary serif.
Interface/metadata: neutral modern sans-serif.
Optional archive notation: restrained monospace/technical face.

Never use ornamental ancient, occult, gothic or pseudo-manuscript typefaces.

## Image Direction
Show artwork as it exists. Prioritize accurate full-work documentation, useful detail crops, authentic installation photography, and studio/process imagery when relevant.

Avoid artificial colour grading, excessive vignette, fake film grain, glowing effects, floating 3D mockups, decorative digital frames and AI-generated environments presented as documentation.

## Scale
Use a rhythm of **Monumental → intimate → documentary → monumental**. Do not make every work the same thumbnail size.

## Negative Space
Whitespace is part of the curatorial experience. Give major works, statements, transitions, metadata and access points room to breathe.

## Layout
Disciplined but not visibly rigid. Use asymmetry, large margins, occasional off-axis placement, editorial columns, full-bleed work and small archival labels. Avoid a conventional three-column gallery grid as the primary experience.

## Motion
Motion behaves like **revelation**: slow opacity transitions, controlled reveals, subtle scale changes, gentle movement. Avoid flashy transitions, scroll-jacking, excessive parallax, bouncing UI and cinematic effects for their own sake.

## Archive Notation
Example:
**VL / SII / B-001**
**SERIES II**
**CHARCOAL ON PAPER**
**STATUS — PRIVATE**

Identifiers should feel documentary rather than fictional. Do not fabricate accession numbers, institutional seals or museum credentials.

## Interface Language
Preferred: View Fragment, Enter Archive, Read Practice, Request Catalogue, Enquire Privately, View Work, Request Viewing.

Avoid: Buy Now, Shop, Unlock the Secret, Discover Your Destiny, Enter the Unknown.

## Three Visual Modes
**Encounter:** large artwork, minimal information, maximum attention.

**Archive:** artwork plus identifiers, metadata and measured documentary information.

**Context:** artist, practice, process, studio and institutional/collector information.

## Art Direction Rules
1. Never decorate an artwork to make it look important.
2. Never allow typography to compete with a major drawing.
3. Never use mythology as visual wallpaper.
4. Never fake institutional authority.
5. Never make every page visually identical.
6. Never reveal the entire archive simply because it exists.
7. Never let acquisition language appear before the work has earned attention.
8. Preserve accurate representation of the physical drawings.
9. Use scarcity through curation and access, not artificial countdowns.
10. If an effect does not improve looking, remove it.

## Art Direction Test
**Does this make the artwork feel more significant, or does it merely make the website look more sophisticated?**

## Working Design North Star
> **A contemporary archive with the silence of a museum, the intimacy of a studio, and the material presence of a sheet of charcoal paper.**
